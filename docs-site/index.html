Introduction

Understand the domain before coding, and prove every rule.

OpenDomain is a domain-driven, evidence-driven development framework designed for working with AI assistants. It turns real-world domain knowledge into validated software: domain modeling, requirements, specifications, test-driven implementation — every step linked to its evidence and checked by quality gates.

The problem

Ask an AI assistant to add invoice management: within seconds you get entities, an API, migrations, and passing tests. The result can still be entirely wrong — wrong tax rate, invented approval workflow, invoice not compliant with the General Tax Code (in Senegal, a standardized invoice must carry a NINEA).

Each generated sentence is plausible in isolation. The defect is not in the code, it is upstream: the assistant cannot tell a reconstructed rule apart from a plausible hallucination, because nothing in the project records what is known, assumed, inferred, or unknown. A human developer hesitates and asks questions when they don't know; the AI's ignorance is syntactically indistinguishable from its competence.

The answer, in three ideas

Say how you know

Every working record declares its knowledge status: observed in an authoritative source (EXPLICIT), known through practice (TACIT), inferred (INFERRED), assumed (ASSUMED), unknown (UNKNOWN), or disputed (CONFLICTING). Seven values, no default. Not knowing becomes a written fact instead of a silence.

Link everything to its evidence

Sources, evidence, business rules, requirements, specifications, tests: all form a single traceability chain. Two questions then become mechanical — why does this code exist? (od trace) and what would this change break? (od impact). It is no longer your memory that answers, it is the graph.

Verify before moving on

Nine quality gates check the work at every stage. The most important one forbids "whitewashing": turning an assumption into a certainty by changing a label, without bringing evidence, is blocked. See The 9 gates.

What the tool does, and doesn't do

The od command reads and checks; it never writes for you. You write the records in Markdown, it tells you whether they hold together — with the exact name of the violated rule and the offending line, never guessing the fix. A demanding reviewer, not an author.

TipStart with Getting started to install the tool and get your first gate verdict, then read Concepts for the substance. The CLI reference and With opencode pages serve daily use.

Going further
