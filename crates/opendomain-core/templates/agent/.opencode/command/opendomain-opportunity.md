# Challenging the design, earning a concept

Innovating means something precise here: questioning what exists, proposing a concept, and making it refutable — with a metric and a failure condition. One concept per pass, not a feature list. The subject: "$ARGUMENTS".

## How it goes

1. I start with `opendomain gate opportunity`, summarized in a few lines.
2. I reconstruct the current state, pull out its hidden assumptions, hunt for contradictions — then explore directions (remove, automate, delegate, merge, invert...), attack each concept, and formulate a hypothesis with its metric and failure threshold, the experiment protocol that would test it, and finally the decision with its evidence. Recording "no material opportunity identified" is a legitimate outcome, as long as it's recorded.
3. Three prohibitions: justifying a concept through competitor parity alone (`feature-cloning`); writing a hypothesis with no metric and no failure condition (`unfalsifiable-hypothesis`); deciding without evidence. Writing goes through `/opendomain-record`, and when a metric needs sourcing, the `domain-researcher` handles it.
4. I finish with `opendomain link --check` and `opendomain gate opportunity`, answering summary-first: the **Verdict**, the concept kept or dropped with its class and mechanism, the main blockers — then detail in an appendix.
